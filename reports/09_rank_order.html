Reports 06 and 07 look at how much each pair's similarity changes. A different question is whether the order of the pairs changes: if pair A is more similar than pair B with APL intact, is it still more similar with APL blocked? A purely global inhibition might be expected to keep the order. This report shows when it does and when it does not, then compares model and data.

What changed in this revision (2026-11-04)

1How a change of order is measured

With 16 odors there are 120 pairs and 7,140 comparisons between two pairs. Spearman ρ\rhoρ correlates the ranks of SonS^{\mathrm{on}}Son and SoffS^{\mathrm{off}}Soff over the pairs. Kendall τ\tauτ counts comparisons directly; without ties,

τ=1−2 # swaps# comparisons,share swapped=1−τ2(1)\tau = 1 - 2\,\frac{\#\,\text{swaps}}{\#\,\text{comparisons}}, \qquad \text{share swapped} = \frac{1 - \tau}{2} \tag{1}τ=1−2#comparisons#swaps​,share swapped=21−τ​(1)

ρ\rhoρ = 1 means no swaps. ρ\rhoρ is less sensitive than τ\tauτ to swaps between pairs with nearly equal similarity.

2When does global inhibition keep the order?

  • Case 1: inhibition only scales. If every KC's response to odor ooo is divided by the same number sos_oso​ (which may differ between odors), cosine similarity does not change at all, because cosine ignores the length of each response vector (Equation 2). The order is kept exactly.
  • Case 2: one threshold raise, odors alike. If inhibition raises one threshold for every odor and KC, and every odor drives the KCs with the same statistics, then similarity depends only on how correlated the two odors' inputs are, through one rising curve at each threshold. The order is kept (up to sampling noise).
  • Case 3: odors differ. If odors differ in strength (3a), the same raise removes a larger share of a weak odor's responders, so pairs of weak odors lose more similarity than pairs of strong ones. If some odors share their overlap mostly in a few strongly driven KCs (3b), those pairs keep more of their overlap. Either way some pairs swap.

3Toy simulations of the three cases

No PN responses and no connectome: 1,000,000 model KCs each get a random input for each of 16 odors. Odors overlap through 4 shared random inputs (each odor takes its own mix) plus a private input. A KC's response is max⁡(input−θ,0)\max(\text{input} - \theta, 0)max(input−θ,0). With APL blocked 20% of KCs respond to an odor on average, and with APL intact 10%. In case 1 the divisor of each odor is 1+aAo1 + a A_o1+aAo​, with AoA_oAo​ the odor's mean response solved self-consistently, and one aaa for all odors set so the intact/blocked ratio averages 0.5.

  • Scaling: every similarity unchanged to 10⁻¹⁴ (ρ\rhoρ = 1).
  • One raise, odors alike: ρ\rhoρ = 0.9998; the 26 swaps are between pairs less than 0.003 apart (sampling noise).
  • Odors differing in strength: ρ\rhoρ = 0.976 (6.1% swapped), and 0.956 (9.0%) with feedback instead of one raise.
  • Sparse shared overlap: ρ\rhoρ = 0.980 (5.6% swapped).
  • A divisor per KC instead of per odor keeps the order when it is random (ρ 0.9999) but not quite when it grows with the number of odors the KC responds to (ρ 0.995).

5The recordings

Over the 120 pairs, TNT and TNT3c similarity give ρ\rhoρ = 0.869, lower than any model version. Part of that is noise: even two sets of recordings of the same condition do not give ρ\rhoρ = 1. To separate the two, the recordings are split at random into halves (5 + 5 of the 11 TNT3c, 4 + 4 of the 8 TNT), 1,000 times. Two halves of the same condition give ρwithin\rho_{\mathrm{within}}ρwithin​; a TNT half against a TNT3c half gives ρbetween\rho_{\mathrm{between}}ρbetween​. If blocking APL kept the order, the two would be equal. Their ratio estimates the rank correlation without noise:

ρtrue≈ρbetweenρwithinTNT  ρwithinTNT3c(3)\rho_{\mathrm{true}} \approx \frac{\rho_{\mathrm{between}}}{\sqrt{\rho^{\mathrm{TNT}}_{\mathrm{within}}\;\rho^{\mathrm{TNT3c}}_{\mathrm{within}}}} \tag{3}ρtrue​≈ρwithinTNT​ρwithinTNT3c​​ρbetween​​(3)
the usual correction for attenuation, applied to rank correlations of half-sample means

5.1The logic of the correction

Equation 3 is Spearman's correction for attenuation. Step by step:

  • 1. Every measurement is pattern + noise. The similarity of a pair measured from a set of recordings is its true value in that condition plus recording noise. A different set of recordings gives the same true value with different noise.
  • 2. Noise lowers correlations. If two measurements XXX and YYY each carry independent noise, their correlation is the correlation of their true values times a shrink factor: corr(X,Y)=corr(TX,TY)⋅relX relY\mathrm{corr}(X, Y) = \mathrm{corr}(T_X, T_Y) \cdot \sqrt{\mathrm{rel}_X\,\mathrm{rel}_Y}corr(X,Y)=corr(TX​,TY​)⋅relX​relY​​, where rel = (variance of the true values) ÷ (variance of the measurement) is the share of a measurement that is not noise.
  • 3. The within-condition correlation measures rel. Two halves of the same condition share the true values and differ only in noise, so their correlation equals rel: ρwithinTNT≈relTNT\rho^{\mathrm{TNT}}_{\mathrm{within}} \approx \mathrm{rel}_{\mathrm{TNT}}ρwithinTNT​≈relTNT​, and the same for TNT3c.
  • 4. Undo the shrink. ρbetween\rho_{\mathrm{between}}ρbetween​ (a TNT half against a TNT3c half) is the true TNT-vs-TNT3c correlation shrunk by relTNT relTNT3c\sqrt{\mathrm{rel}_{\mathrm{TNT}}\,\mathrm{rel}_{\mathrm{TNT3c}}}relTNT​relTNT3c​​. Dividing by that factor gives Equation 3.
  • 5. Why halves on both sides. The between value is computed from half-size samples, exactly like the within values, so both carry the same amount of noise and no Spearman–Brown step is needed.
  • ρbetween\rho_{\mathrm{between}}ρbetween​ = 0.831 vs ρwithin\rho_{\mathrm{within}}ρwithin​ = 0.904 (TNT3c) and 0.906 (TNT); the between value is lower than both within values in 97.5% of splits.
  • Corrected for noise, ρtrue\rho_{\mathrm{true}}ρtrue​ ≈ 0.92. The model versions give 0.94–0.98, so the recordings reorder odor pairs more than uniform or connectome-weighted feedback inhibition on the KCs predicts.
  • Counting only responses above 1 (0/1) weakens the effect: 0.85 between vs 0.88 and 0.89 within, lower in 81% of splits.

What could reorder more

Inhibition that changes the input pattern differently for each odor, rather than one threshold per odor. In the model, the version that reorders most is P (ρ = 0.943). Report 11 tests a local, non-spiking APL on KCs and boutons.

Differences between the recorded flies and the connectome flies, or response nonlinearities not in the model.
